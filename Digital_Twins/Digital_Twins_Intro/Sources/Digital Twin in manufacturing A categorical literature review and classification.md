## Source Summary
A landmark academic review paper that resolved widespread ambiguity in Industry 4.0 literature by establishing the canonical **data integration taxonomy** separating digital models, digital shadows, and digital twins:

1. **The Three-Tier Data Flow Classification:**
   - **Digital Model:** Characterized by completely **manual data exchange** in both directions. Any update from the physical asset to the digital representation (or changes executed back on the physical entity) requires human intervention. Examples include standard 3D CAD assemblies, static FEA/CFD simulations, and factory architectural blueprints.
   - **Digital Shadow:** Characterized by an **automated one-way data flow** originating from the physical object to the digital replica, but with manual feedback to the physical system. Telemetry and sensor feeds update the virtual model continuously, yet the virtual model cannot automatically command or modify physical actuators. Examples include live operational dashboards, telemetry loggers, and condition-monitoring platforms.
   - **Digital Twin:** Characterized by **fully automated, closed-loop, bidirectional data exchange**. Real-time sensor streams update the virtual state dynamically, and optimization outputs/control signals generated by simulation and machine learning models are sent directly back to actuators or control logic (PLCs) without mandatory human intermediation.

2. **Literature Gap Analysis:** Surveyed state-of-the-art publications up to 2018 and proved that the majority of industry use cases claiming to be "Digital Twins" were technically only *Digital Models* or *Digital Shadows* due to the absence of the automated return-control loop.

3. **Relevance for Foundational Research:** Delivers mathematical and structural precision to prove why a 3D visualization or an isolated simulation run cannot be classified as a true Digital Twin.
