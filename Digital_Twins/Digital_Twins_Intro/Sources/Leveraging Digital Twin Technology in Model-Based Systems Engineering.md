## Source Summary
An academic paper that positions the digital twin within Model-Based Systems Engineering and proposes a ladder of increasing twin capability:

1. **Definition:** A digital twin is a virtual instance of a physical system that is kept up to date with the system's performance, maintenance and health-status data across its life cycle.
2. **Four levels (Table 1):**
   - **Pre-Digital Twin:** a virtual prototype built during upfront engineering; no physical twin exists yet.
   - **Digital Twin:** a physical twin exists and the model receives performance, health and maintenance data in batch updates.
   - **Adaptive Digital Twin:** real-time updates plus supervised (neural-network-based) learning of operator preferences.
   - **Intelligent Digital Twin:** adds environment data and unsupervised/reinforcement learning of system and environment behavior.
3. **Key differentiators:** Existence of the physical twin, data update mode (batch vs. real time) and scope of machine learning.
4. **Relevance:** Provides an engineering-oriented maturity ladder that complements Arup's multi-metric framework and the Kritzinger integration taxonomy. Note that its use of the term "Digital Twin" at Level 2 is looser than Kritzinger et al.'s definition.
