# [Source] - Draft Space Technology Roadmap: Modeling, Simulation, Information Technology & Processing (TA11) | NASA

- **Author/Entity:** NASA Office of the Chief Technologist (OCT) / Lead: John Vickers et al.
- **Year:** 2010 (Published November 2010)
- **Focus:** Foundational Engineering, Physics-based Multiscale Modeling, Spaceflight Missions
- **Key Quotes:**
  - *"A Digital Twin is an integrated multiphysics, multiscale, probabilistic simulation of a vehicle or system that uses the best available physical models, sensor updates, fleet history, etc., to mirror the life of its flying twin."*
  - *"The Digital Twin is ultra-realistic and may consider one or more of the vehicle's systems (e.g., thermal protection, structural, propulsion, avionics, life support)."*
  - *"In addition to mirroring the life of its twin, the Digital Twin will continuously forecast the health of the vehicle/system, the remaining useful life, and the probability of mission success."*
  - *"The Digital Twin will also adapt to operational changes through self-learning and act as a decision support system during anomalous flight events."*

## Source Summary
A foundational roadmap document in which NASA formally introduced and defined the term **Digital Twin** for aerospace applications (Technology Area 11: Modeling, Simulation, Information Technology & Processing). Key takeaways include:

1. **Formal Definition & Scope:** Defined the Digital Twin as an ultra-high-fidelity, probabilistic, multiphysics simulation that continuously mirrors a specific flight vehicle throughout its entire operational lifecycle using real-time telemetry and flight history.
2. **Beyond Standard CAD/Simulations:** Shifted engineering focus from generic nominal design models to individual asset tracking, incorporating real-world manufacturing deviations, material anomalies, environmental degradation, and thermal loads.
3. **Mission Success & Contingency Operations:** Outlined how a flying vehicle's virtual twin operates as an onboard or ground-based autonomous decision-support system, diagnosing unpredicted anomalies and predicting Remaining Useful Life (RUL) when physical inspections are impossible.
4. **Computational Requirements:** Identified essential enabling capabilities, including petascale/exascale computing, Bayesian uncertainty quantification, embedded structural health monitoring (SHM) sensors, and multiscale physics models.
