# [Source] - Digital Twin: Towards a Meaningful Framework | Arup

- **Author/Entity:** Arup (engineering and design consultancy)
- **Year:** 2019 (released November 2019)
- **Focus:** Definitions, an evaluation framework for twin sophistication, and value across cities, energy, property, transport and water
- **Key Quote:**
  - *"the combination of a computational model and a real-world system"* (from Arup's definition of a digital twin)

## Source Summary
An industry report whose stated goal is to align terminology around digital twins and offer a working framework for categorizing how sophisticated a given twin is:

1. **Definition and boundaries:** A digital twin couples a computational model with a real-world system in order to monitor, control and optimize it. A model or simulation that operates in isolation from its physical asset (the report's example is a BIM model) does not qualify.
2. **Four evaluation metrics (Table 2):** Autonomy, intelligence, learning and fidelity, each scored from 1 to 5. The metrics are presented as conceptually related but independent, and the required fidelity depends on the asset operator's needs.
3. **Five levels of sophistication (Table 3):** From a digital model linked to a real system without intelligence, learning or autonomy (Level 1), through feedback and control (Level 2), predictive maintenance and analytics (Level 3) and domain-specific autonomous decisions (Level 4), to a twin that approaches autonomous reasoning and integrates lower-level twins (Level 5).
4. **State of the field (2019):** Most twins lack the ability to learn and reason; twins at Levels 4 and 5 are still rare. The report envisions an ecosystem of twins that share data and learn from each other across boundaries.
5. **Market chapters:** Case studies in cities, energy, property, transport and water are each evaluated with the framework.
