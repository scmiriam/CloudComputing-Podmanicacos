## Source Summary
A seminal conceptual text expanding Michael Grieves's original 2002–2003 PLM premise into a comprehensive theoretical framework for managing complex physical systems:

1. **The Core Triad Model:** Establishes the canonical three-element architecture:
   - **Real Space:** The physical product and its operational environment.
   - **Virtual Space:** The informational representation and computational physics model.
   - **Data Links:** The continuous dual-channel connection (data flowing from real to virtual space; controls, visualizations, and parameters flowing from virtual back to real space).
2. **Typological Distinction (DTP vs. DTI vs. DTA):**
   - **Digital Twin Prototype (DTP):** The archetype model created during design/R&D (the "ideal" blueprint before serial manufacturing).
   - **Digital Twin Instance (DTI):** The dedicated twin tied to a single, specific serial-numbered physical unit, retaining its unique telemetry, wear, and operating history.
   - **Digital Twin Aggregate (DTA):** The collective compilation of all DTIs across a fleet, leveraged to detect macro-level trends, anomalies, and aggregate learning.
3. **Mitigating Undesirable Emergent Behavior:** Explains that as systems scale in complexity, interactions between parts produce behaviors unobservable in isolated testing. High-fidelity virtual execution allows engineers to surface hidden failure modes, micro-cracks, and thermal drift virtually before catastrophic physical failure occurs.
4. **Information-Resource Trade-off:** Formulates the foundational economic thesis of digital twins: replacing expensive physical resources (time, energy, materials, hardware trials) with lower-cost digital information and compute.
